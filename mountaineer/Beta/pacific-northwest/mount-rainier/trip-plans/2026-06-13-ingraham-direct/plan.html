Route Overview

Primary routeParadise TH (5,400 ft) → Muir Snowfield → Camp Muir (10,188 ft) → Cathedral Gap (10,880 ft) → Ingraham Flats (11,100 ft) → Ingraham Direct up glacier — 3 ladder crossings (11,600–13,200 ft) → summit plateau → Columbia Crest (14,411 ft)
DescentSame route reversed. Record GPS ascent track and use on descent — fresh snow covers boot packs; multiple tracks lead to dead ends at open crevasses.
Bail / turnaroundReverse ascent route to Paradise from any point. Camp Muir is the primary refuge if weather turns. Do not unrope until below Cathedral Gap. Do not attempt DC or alternate descent without prior familiarity.
Key hazards (1) Crevasse zone 11,400–13,200 ft: 3 ladder crossings; bridges weaken after sunrise.
(2) Wind slab ~12,400 ft from June 7–9 storm: largely settled by June 15 (6 days) — assess surface before committing above.
(3) Fresh snow obscuring boot packs — navigation error risk on descent.
(4) Serac / rockfall in upper Ingraham exposure zones — move through continuously, no stopping.
(5) Summit temp near freezing (32–35°F): snow bridges deteriorate faster — cross before 11:00 AM absolute limit.
(6) Afternoon snow softening — slow descent, no glissade likely.
(7) False summit before Columbia Crest — brief team on route.
Reassessment pointsCamp Muir · Ingraham Flats · 12,400 ft (slab check) · 13,200 ft (hard time check)

Field Abort Criteria

  • Any person calls stop or requests turnaround — immediate compliance, no debate
  • Sustained wind >35 mph at the team's current elevation
  • Any precipitation begins (rain at any elevation; significant snow above 11,000 ft)
  • Visibility drops below 100 ft; whiteout conditions
  • Any crevasse fall, even self-rescued — reassess before continuing
  • Active avalanche in route corridor, or cracking / whumpfing at 11,400 ft+
  • AMS symptoms in any member above 10,000 ft (headache + nausea, ataxia, behavioral change)
  • Snow bridges sound hollow, sag, or cannot be probed safe — reroute or turn around
  • Missed Gate 3: not at 13,200 ft by 7:30 AM — turn around immediately
  • Hard turnaround: 10:00 AM regardless of elevation
  • Serac or rockfall in route corridor — stop; wait minimum 15 min; then retreat or detour

Rope Teams and Glacier / Crevasse Plan

Configuration: 2 rope teams of 2, each on a 60 m rope. Rope length check: 1 interval × 12.5 m + 5 m knots + 15 m rescue reserve = 32.5 m minimum. 60 m rope provides ~42 m free after spacing — sufficient 3:1 haul reserve for 5 m crevasse depth. Stronger navigator leads Team 1.
RoleTeam 1Team 2
Leader (front)Climber AClimber C
SecondClimber BClimber D
Rope-up pointNo later than Cathedral Gap (10,880 ft). Rope up earlier if any crevasse hazard visible before Muir.
Rope-off pointDo not unrope until below Cathedral Gap on the Camp Muir side. Ice-rock interfaces at Muir are active crevasse zones.
Spacing12–15 m between climbers; butterfly knots every ~4 m; rope taut during bridge crossings.
Snow bridge strategyOne climber crosses at a time. Remaining climber braced and self-arrested, rope taut. Probe suspect bridges with avalanche probe before committing. If hollow, sagging, or undercut — reroute.
Ladder crossingsOne person at a time; rest of rope team stationary and braced. Do not stack rope on ladder rungs.
Crevasse fall responseArrest immediately and brace. Stop movement. Build 2-picket anchor. Communicate. Assess injury. Attempt Z-haul self-rescue. If unsuccessful or patient injured: activate inReach SOS, signal other rope team, insulate patient, await NPS rescue.
If leader incapacitatedStop movement. Hold and anchor rope. Activate inReach SOS. Signal other rope team and nearby parties. Insulate patient from snow.
NavigationRecord GPS ascent track and use on descent to avoid boot-pack dead ends. Primary: GPS with GPX. Secondary: paper topo + compass + altimeter. Build whiteout bearings at Camp Muir.
