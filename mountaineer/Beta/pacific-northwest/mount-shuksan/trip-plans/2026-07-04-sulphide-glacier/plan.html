Route Overview

Item Details
Primary route Shannon Ridge Trail (old road → climbers path) to NPS boundary ~4,600 ft → broken slabs to lower glacier/last water ~6,200 ft → low-angle Sulphide Glacier to base of summit pyramid ~8,600 ft → Class 3–4 central snow gully to summit 9,131 ft
Descent Reverse the ascent line (record the track up). Summit-block descent — decide at the top of the gully: if it is good downclimbable snow (likely, per NPS 6/15 "gully still snow") and you feel solid → downclimb face-in with crampons + axe (lightest, no anchor to trust). If it is icy/verglassed, dry-loose rock, or you are tired → rappel off the anchor, backed up (experience log: fixed pins unreliable — bring 2–3 slings + a nut/small cam or a picket for a snow bollard/picket anchor, plus cord). A 30 m rope gives a ~15 m doubled rappel — enough for the steep step; downclimb the easier Class 3 below. On the glacier the team travels roped; unrope at the base of the pyramid and belay/rappel the rock.
Bail / turnaround route Descend the ascent route. From anywhere on the glacier, retreat to 6,200 ft and out. Do not commit to the pyramid if late or socked in.
Key hazards Crevasse exposure; exposed loose Class 3–4 gully; afternoon snow softening; whiteout route-finding

Food / Water / Waste Plan

Water — the core of your minimal-carry question

Your ~5–6 L daily need does not mean carrying 5–6 L uphill. The route lets you refill low, so you only ever haul water across the one dry segment: 6,200 ft → summit → 6,200 ft (no reliable water above 6,200 ft, per NPS 2026-06-15).

Item Plan
Starting water ~1.5 L off the car. The night/early-morning approach is cool → low sweat rate, so you don't need more low down.
Refill point(s) (1) Approach streams on the Shannon Ridge trail (abundant early season) — filter and drink freely, don't carry it. (2) Last running water ~6,200 ft near the lower bivy (NPS confirms running water here) — this is the critical top-off. (3) Same spot on descent for the hot forest walk-out.
The one carry-up load At 6,200 ft, drink ~0.5 L on the spot ("camel up") and fill to ~3 L for the ~5 h summit round trip. That ~3 L is the most you ever carry — everything below is drink-and-refill.
Water cache (your idea) The smart cache is not at 6,200 ft (running water there — just refill). Cache ~1–1.5 L at the base of the summit pyramid (~8,600 ft) — a rocky, crevasse-free transition/rest spot, easy to relocate (you're stopping there to gear up for the gully anyway). You then climb the exposed gully carrying only ~1.5 L, and grab the cache on the way down for the glacier walk back to 6,200 ft. Mark it (bright bottle / GPS point) and set it where it won't slide or bury.
Daily budget ~5–6 L total for the day, but split: ~2 L below 6,200 ft (drink-and-refill), ~3 L on the summit push, ~1 L on the walk-out (refilled at 6,200 ft).
Electrolytes Required, not optional. Drinking 5–6 L of plain water risks hyponatremia. Carry salt/electrolyte tabs (e.g., 1 tab per liter on the climb) and/or salty foods.
Filter / treatment Light squeeze filter (Katadyn BeFree / Sawyer) — ~60–90 g. This replaces carried weight: it's what lets you start with only 1.5 L.
Stove for melt Leave it (you asked for minimal). Not needed early July with running water at 6,200 ft + streams below. Contingency if 6,200 ft is unexpectedly dry: filter from glacier melt runnels, or eat snow sparingly.

Net answer: carry ~1.5 L from the car, refill/filter at streams, top off to ~3 L at 6,200 ft, then cache ~1–1.5 L at the pyramid base (8,600 ft) so the gully is light. Refill at 6,200 ft again on the way down. Peak carried weight ≈ 3 L (~3 kg), and only ~1.5 L on the technical ground — never the full 5–6 L.

Glacier / Crevasse Travel Plan

Item Plan
Core principle Roped team travel across the Sulphide, plus timing and probing. Be on/over the bridged sections while frozen (pre-dawn to mid-morning); off them before afternoon warming.
Crevasse depth / why this matters Sulphide crevasses are deep — commonly 10–30 m (30–100 ft), sometimes more — under snow bridges that may be only 1–2 m thick in early July. Low angle does not mean shallow. A fall through a bridge can be incapacitating even where the surface looks benign — hence roped travel plus prevention (frozen timing + probing).
Rope-up / rope-off Rope up where the snow becomes continuous (~6,200 ft) and stay roped until off the glacier on descent. Keep the rope taut; friction knots between climbers; ~10–12 m spacing on this low-angle terrain.
Snow bridge strategy Probe suspect bridges before weighting; cross one at a time with the rope taut and the rest of the team braced and ready to arrest; follow but verify the boot track; if hollow/sagging/undercut, reroute or retreat.
Crevasse fall response Team self-arrest, anchor the rope (picket), then assisted haul (Z-pulley) or the fallen climber ascends the rope via prusik/ascender; inReach SOS if needed.
Navigation Preloaded GPX on 2 devices + record the ascent track for a whiteout descent; do not descend an unfamiliar line blind.
Other parties Holiday weekend — expect a few guided/independent parties; note their location for mutual aid.
Turnaround The summit is optional and the deadline is fixed. Turning around with soft bridges or poor visibility is the correct, planned outcome.
