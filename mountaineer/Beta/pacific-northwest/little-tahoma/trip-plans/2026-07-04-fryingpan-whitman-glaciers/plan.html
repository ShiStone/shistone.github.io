Little Tahoma – Fryingpan / Whitman Glaciers (East Shoulder)

Trip date: July 4, 2026 (depart) → July 5, 2026 (summit) — 2-day overnight
Objective style: Overnight glacier climb. Day 1 approach to high camp; Day 2 summit and full exit. Summit is optional; safe return is primary.
Team: Mixed party of experienced climbers and beginners.
Plan status: Drafted June 30, 2026.
Verify before departure: NOAA/Windy weather and freezing level, road/parking status, permit reservation + in-person validation, Fryingpan Creek bridge, and current crevasse/rockfall conditions must be re-checked on July 2 and again the morning of departure.
Two camp scenarios in this plan. Primary = high camp ~8,900 ft on the Whitman Glacier (just past Whitman Notch). Backup = Meany Crest ~7,400 ft. Each has its own Day 1 carry and its own Day 2 alpine-start time (see Route Timing). The higher camp gives a shorter summit-day push and gets the party off the loose summit block and steep snow before afternoon warming, at the cost of a longer, heavier Day 1.

Freezing-level implication (the dominant driver). The summit sits ~1,500 ft below the freezing level, so daytime is near/above freezing and overnight refreeze is marginal. The firm-snow window is short: climb the steep snow firm, summit early, and be off the glacier and loose rock before midday. Summit day (Sunday) is the calmer of the two — good for the exposed upper ridge.

The dawn column confirms the marginal overnight refreeze (barely sub-freezing at camp and the headwall) — reinforcing the early start so the steep snow is climbed firm.

Cloud / whiteout: Both days the sounding shows a dry column, cloud base well above the summit, and no inversion → whiteout risk LOW. Trend to watch on the Windy Clouds/Sounding layers: afternoon convective cumulus building over the massif on warm days; if the model adds mid-level moisture, expect afternoon cloud — another reason to summit and descend before midday.

Risk Assessment

Synthesized from past trip reports — cwillett route notes, TrailCatJim 2023, climberkyle 2022, turns-all-year (Frying Pan Creek), SummitPost route page, Eric's Base Camp — combined with the current dry/warm/high-freezing-level forecast. Common thread: nearly every hazard here is amplified by afternoon warmth, so the master mitigation is to be high early and down off steep/loose terrain before midday.

Avalanche: LOW / negligible (slab). NWAC's daily avalanche products have ended for the season; there is no recent storm snow or wind-loading, and the snowpack is summer-consolidated. The only snow-instability concern is afternoon wet-loose on steep sun-baked aspects, controlled by early timing, not rescue gear. (One shoulder-season report flagged avalanche risk "with new snow expected" — a storm cycle that does not apply to the current dry, warm pattern.) Conclusion: avalanche beacons are not warranted for these dates. Carry a shovel and probe per rope team for crevasse rescue and snow-bridge probing only.

Route Overview

ItemDetails
Primary routeFryingpan Creek Trail → Summerland (~5,900 ft) → climb the snow slog to Meany Crest (~7,400 ft) (a deep bergschrund forms on this slope — pass it carefully) → rope up, ascend Fryingpan Glacier to Whitman Notch (~9,000–9,100 ft) → descend onto the Whitman Glacier, camp ~8,900 ft → ascend steep firm snow above the bergschrund to the base of the upper snow / summit headwall (~10,400 ft) → steep snow (~40°) to the small V left of the summit → loose Class 3 rock scramble to summit (11,138 ft).
DescentReverse the route. Stay roped from the summit-block base across both glaciers and back over the notch to Meany Crest. Glissade only where slope, runout, and crevasse exposure are confirmed safe.
Bail / turnaroundTurn at the base of the upper snow (~10,400 ft) if the summit block is actively shedding rock, too loose/exposed for the party, or behind the turnaround clock — still a safe, successful glacier high point. From anywhere on the glacier, descend the ascent track roped.
Reassessment pointsSummerland (weather/conditions), Meany Crest (rope-up readiness, glacier entrance), Whitman Notch ~9,000 ft (turnaround clock, snow/crevasse state), base of upper snow ~10,400 ft (rockfall + go/no-go on summit block).

Route Timing

Pace plan. Day 1 carries heavy overnight packs (~40 lb): expect ~700 ft·hr⁻¹ on trail and steep snow including transitions — benchmarked to a reported ~6.5 hr trailhead→Meany Crest with overnight loads (TrailCatJim). Day 2 uses light summit packs: ~900 ft·hr⁻¹ on firm AM snow, ~500 ft·hr⁻¹ on the loose Class 3 block. Fixed transitions: rope-up + crampons ~30 min, breaks. Before Leaving

  • Permits in hand (wilderness camp zone + $82 climbing fee), validated at White River WIC.
  • Weather/freezing-level scored Climb or Caution; overnight refreeze expected.
  • Road open and parking plan set; satellite messenger tested, contacts loaded, overdue plan shared with off-trip contact.
  • All crampons fitted to boots; rope teams and rescue kits assigned.

Field Abort Criteria

  • Any person calls stop or requests turnaround — immediate compliance.
  • Not at the base of the upper snow (~10,400 ft) by 7:00 AM, or 9:00 AM hard turnaround reached.
  • Active rockfall/icefall observed in the summit-block corridor.
  • Any crevasse fall (even self-rescued); snow bridges/bergschrund found hollow/sagging with no safe reroute.
  • Sustained wind > 30 mph at the party's elevation, precip begins, or visibility < 100 ft on the glacier.
  • AMS symptoms (headache + nausea or ataxia) in any member above 10,000 ft.
