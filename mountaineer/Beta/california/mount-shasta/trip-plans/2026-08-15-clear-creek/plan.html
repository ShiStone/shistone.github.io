Route Overview

ItemDetails
Primary routeClear Creek route, southeast side. Trail → Clear Creek springs/meadow → open east slopes → braided scree to Mushroom Rock → headwall (middle Class 2–3 line) → summit plateau → summit.
DescentSame route. Middle Class 2–3 line back to Mushroom Rock, then descend climbers' right for plunge-stepping on scree, traverse back to the meadow, trail to trailhead.
Bail / turnaround routeDescend the ascent route. There is no shorter alternative; the route is a single ridge with the trailhead at its base. Bailing from anywhere above the meadow is a straightforward reversal on the same ground.
Key hazardsAltitude with zero acclimatization; night navigation on featureless scree; loose rock in the headwall; cold while stationary on the summit.
Reassessment pointsClear Creek spring (~8,400 ft, water and layers); 10,000 ft sunrise stop; 11,500 ft (altitude check); Mushroom Rock ~12,900 ft — checkpoint gate 10:45; base of summit plateau.
Terrain angle / transitions~15% grade on trail to 8,400 ft; ~31% sustained above the meadow; steepest and loosest above 12,800 ft. No crampon transition expected — microspikes on only if an ice patch demands it. Mountaineering boots not required; sturdy boots suffice for a melted-out route.
Rock protectionNone needed. The headwall middle line is Class 2–3 scrambling, downclimbed on descent. No rope, rack, slings, or rappel anchors. If a party finds itself wanting protection, it is on the Class 4 western variant and should reverse and re-find the middle line.
Required team skillsComfortable unroped on loose Class 2–3 rock, ascending and downclimbing; sustained scree travel and plunge-stepping; night navigation from a GPS track over featureless terrain; self-sufficiency at 14,000 ft with no acclimatization. No glacier, crevasse-rescue, self-arrest, or technical rock skills are required. Unsuitable for inexperienced hikers — the altitude, the 16 hr duration, and the all-night navigation are the barriers, not the climbing.
Fall consequenceHeadwall: a slip on loose Class 2–3 has real injury consequence but no long runout to a cliff. Elsewhere a slip on scree is low-consequence. The party is 2 people at night in a place with almost no other traffic until well after dawn — an immobilizing injury is a satellite-SOS event, not a walk-out.

Route Timing

Distance — measured

The 16 mi figure from The Mountaineers and some guide services includes forest road below the trailhead — the CalTopo track begins before the trailhead, and Gaia carries a "Clear Creek Trail + Forest Road 41N25Y" variant at 9.2 mi one way. If the last road mile is undrivable and you park low, that is the day you have bought: leave an hour earlier and expect a road walk at the end.

Why sunrise no longer requires the summit

The route climbs Shasta's east face, so the eastern horizon is open from the moment the party clears treeline around 8,500–9,000 ft. Sunrise is visible from anywhere on the open scree above that — it is not a summit-only event. The plan therefore targets ~10,000 ft at sunrise, which removes the two worst features of an all-night summit-at-dawn push: ten hours of headlamp travel, and ~70 minutes stationary on a 14,179 ft summit at roughly 15 °F wind chill.

The trade this schedule makes

Moving sunrise off the summit buys a night's sleep, cuts headlamp travel from ~10 hr to ~3¾ hr, and removes the stationary summit freeze. It pays for that with an afternoon descent on a dry, shadeless east face:

  • The dry segment above the spring now runs 04:25 to 16:07 — about 11¾ hours, much of it in full sun. This is why the water carry went from 4 L to 5–6 L plus a cache.
  • Afternoon thunderstorm exposure. A 10–15% chance of afternoon storms was in the regional forecast pattern. Being at 12,900 ft at 14:00 on an open slope is worse than being at 12,900 ft at 04:00.
  • Heat-driven rockfall in the headwall is more likely at 13:52 than before dawn.

Field Abort Criteria

  • Any person calls stop or requests turnaround — immediate compliance.
  • Missed checkpoint gate: not at Mushroom Rock (12,900 ft) by 10:45.
  • Hard turnaround 13:00, wherever the team stands.
  • AMS symptoms above 10,000 ft: headache with nausea, or any ataxia — descend immediately.
  • Sustained wind above 30 mph at the party's elevation.
  • Any precipitation, or lightning/thunder heard.
  • Visibility below 100 ft on the upper slope — the terrain is featureless and the GPX is the only handrail.
  • Building cumulus over the summit or thunder heard — descend immediately, this is an open slope with no shelter.
  • Rockfall observed in the headwall corridor.
  • Headlamp or navigation-device failure without a working spare.
  • Smoke thick enough to cause respiratory symptoms in either climber.

Glacier / Crevasse Travel Plan

Emergency Protocol

StepAction
1Stop movement. Move out of the fall line and out from under any party above.
2Administer first aid. Insulate the patient from the ground immediately — cold injury compounds fast at altitude at night.
3Determine: self-rescue, assisted rescue (signal other parties — this route sees traffic in daylight, almost none at night), or emergency callout.
4Call 911 if cell available. Otherwise activate satellite SOS. Provide: GPS coordinates, number of patients, injuries, team size, weather, terrain type, communication capability, current actions.
5Notify emergency contact and the ranger station via satellite message once stable.
6Helicopter response: secure loose gear, mark a clear LZ if terrain allows, protect eyes and ears, do not approach until crew directs.
